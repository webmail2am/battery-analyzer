Quick start

  1. Put the cell in the holder and switch the analyzer on (SW1). For 2 seconds the screen shows SETTINGS: the stored battery type and cutoff voltage. Then it shows READY, the cell voltage, and the selected mode.
  2. Connect the USB-serial adapter to the PC. Open this file in Chrome or Edge.
  3. Choose the baud rate for your Pro Mini: 38400 for the LGT8F328P, 57600 for an original ATmega328P. Click Connect and pick the COM port. Live readings appear within a few seconds.
  4. Pick a mode (QUICK, FULL, CHG+DIS, OUTPUT) and click Start test, or use the buttons on the analyzer.
  5. When the test ends, the Test result card appears with the full summary. Use Export report or Export CSV to save it. The result is also shown on the analyzer and added to the Results table.
  6. Testing a LiFePO4 or LiHV cell? Set the battery type first (Calibration & settings).
  7. Switch SW1 off when you're done. The electronics keep drawing about 30–60 mA from the cell.

No hardware at hand? Click Demo. It simulates a 2600 mAh cell with time running 60× faster.

The analyzer

It measures one Li-ion cell through a 0.02 Ω shunt in the cell's minus lead with a 16-bit AD7705 ADC. Everything the cell supplies is measured: the load, the analyzer's own electronics (it runs from the cell through a 5 V boost and a 3.3 V regulator), and charge current (shown negative).

Cell voltage0–5 V~0.1 mV

Connections

  • Cell: the cell holder. Use thick wires for the load path. The voltage sense wire and the cell-minus wire go to the AD7705 module on their own thin wires.
  • Load terminals: a power resistor (2–5 Ω, power rated) for battery tests, or the device to power in OUTPUT mode. Device + goes to BAT+, device − to the load terminal (MOSFET).
  • USB 5 V input: only needed for charging (CHG+DIS mode).
  • Serial header: GND, TX, RX, DTR to the USB-serial adapter. Don't connect its VCC while the analyzer runs from the cell.

Test modes

Rint: internal resistance

The load is off for 1 s (open-circuit voltage Voc), then on for 1 s (loaded voltage and current). Rint = (Voc − Vload) / (Iload − Iidle). Typical values: a good 18650 is 20–60 mΩ, a worn one 100 mΩ or more. Contact and holder resistance count too, so press the cell firmly into the holder.

QUICK: capacity estimate (about 5–45 minutes)

  1. Measures Rint and the resting voltage OCV₀.
  2. Discharges until the estimated resting voltage (V + I × Rint) has dropped 60 mV: at least 1 minute, at most 30. The screen shows the progress as dV 23/60mV.
  3. Rests until the voltage has settled (rises less than 1 mV per minute), 3 to 15 minutes, then measures OCV₁.
  4. If the resting voltage moved less than 40 mV, it does one more drain-and-rest round (title "QUICK DRAIN 2").
  5. Estimates capacity from the change in state of charge: mAh ÷ (SoC(OCV₀) − SoC(OCV₁)).

The result says how reliable it is: Approx +-5% (voltage moved 50 mV or more), +-10% (30–50 mV) or Low confidence. Small cells and heavy loads finish in a few minutes. Large cells in the flat middle of their curve take longest. Best accuracy comes from a load of about 0.5–1C (for a 500 mAh cell, 250–500 mA). On LiFePO4 the voltage curve is almost flat, so QUICK is only a rough guide there: use FULL or a full charge-discharge.

FULL: discharge to cutoff

Measures Rint, then discharges until the voltage under load stays below the cutoff voltage (see Battery settings). The result shows capacity (mAh), energy (Wh), time, average current and Rint. For a true capacity, start with a fully charged cell, or use CHG+DIS.

CHG+DIS: charge, rest, discharge

Li-ion / LiPo only: the TP4056 charges to 4.2 V, so this mode is disabled for LiHV and LiFePO4. Charges with the TP4056 until it reports full (needs USB 5 V), rests 10 minutes, measures Rint, then runs a FULL discharge. Afterwards it recharges the cell while showing the result. This gives the most reliable capacity figure.

OUTPUT mode: power and measure a device

  1. Connect the device to the load terminals instead of the load resistor: device + to BAT+, device − to the load terminal.
  2. Select OUTPUT with MODE (or the OUTPUT button here), then press START (Output ON).
  3. The analyzer first measures its own current with the output off, then switches the output on. All readings show the device's share: current, power, mAh, Wh, minimum and maximum current.
  4. Press START or RINT (Output OFF) to stop. The output also switches off at the cutoff voltage, above 2.5 A, or after 20 h. The result gives energy, run time, and average / minimum / maximum current.

The device gets the cell voltage (4.2 → 3.0 V). Below about 20 mA the reading is rough, because the analyzer's own consumption varies by a few mA. Very short current pulses are averaged.

Using this software

Connecting

  • Works in Chrome and Edge (Web Serial). Firefox and Safari can't open serial ports. It also works offline.
  • Connecting restarts the analyzer, as an upload does. Connect before starting a long test.
  • If nothing appears after 10 seconds, the badge shows No data and the Console says why (see Troubleshooting).

Live readings and control

The left panel shows voltage, current, power, capacity, energy, internal resistance, state of charge (only meaningful at rest), the test state and the test time. The mode buttons, Start test, Rint test and Abort / Back / Output OFF do the same as the analyzer's buttons.

Charts

  • V & I vs time: voltage (left axis) and current (right axis).
  • V vs capacity: the discharge curve, voltage against mAh.
  • V–I curve: every reading as a point. Each load step (the load switching on or off) is drawn as an orange segment with its resistance ΔV/ΔI; the median of all steps is shown below the chart.
  • Hover on a chart for exact values. Clear empties it ("clear on test start" does this automatically). Export CSV saves the raw readings, Save PNG saves the chart image.

Test result

When a test finishes, the Test result card appears (highlighted) in the left panel. It shows:

  • Capacity (or the QUICK estimate, or what an OUTPUT device used), energy, discharge or run time, and average current
  • Internal resistance (OUTPUT: minimum and maximum current)
  • Voltage at the start and end
  • How it ended, and the battery type and cutoff used
  • Export report: saves an HTML page with the summary and the charts of this test. Open it in the browser to read or print it, or choose Print → Save as PDF for a PDF.
  • Export CSV: saves the summary followed by every reading of this test (time, V, A, mAh, mWh, state), for Excel.

Start and end voltage and the charts need the readings, so keep the PC connected for the whole test.

Results

Each finished test and Rint measurement is added to the table and kept in this browser on this PC, together with the test's readings.

  • Click a row to show that result again in the Test result card, with its charts and both exports. The row stays highlighted.
  • Export saves the whole table as CSV. Clear deletes all results and their stored readings.
  • Results saved by older versions of this program open without charts or start/end voltage.

Console

Shows the analyzer's messages (live data lines are hidden). Type a command and press Enter; see Serial protocol.

Battery settings: type and cutoff voltage

Open Calibration & settings, choose the battery type, check the cutoff voltage (the voltage where discharges end), and click Save to analyzer. They're stored on the analyzer's chip and kept after power-off. The idle screen shows them, e.g. Li-ion/LiPo 3.00V.

The battery type also selects the voltage-to-charge table used for the SoC reading and the QUICK estimate. NiMH, NiCd and LTO cells can't be tested: the analyzer runs from the cell under test, and its boost converter needs at least about 2 V.

Firmware update (without VS Code)

This program can put the analyzer firmware on a Pro Mini by itself. It carries the compiled firmware for both boards.

  1. Wire the USB-serial adapter as for normal use, including DTR, and set it to 3.3 V. Close any other serial monitor (VS Code, Arduino IDE).
  2. Open Firmware update, choose the board (read the marking on the chip: LGT8F328P or ATMEGA328P), and keep Bundled firmware (or pick a .hex file built elsewhere).
  3. Click Flash firmware, pick the COM port, and confirm. The board restarts into its bootloader. The program writes all pages, reads them back to verify (about 10–20 seconds), then reconnects at the right baud rate.
  • No bootloader found: check the DTR wire. Without DTR, press the board's reset button right when "Resetting the board…" appears.
  • Blank chips: a brand-new Pro Mini already has its bootloader. A truly blank ATmega328P chip needs the bootloader burned once with an ISP programmer (USBasp or "Arduino as ISP"); after that it can be flashed here.
  • Battery type, cutoff and other stored settings are kept; only the program memory is replaced.

Calibration

Do this once after building, and after changing the shunt, the AD7705 module or the reference. You need a multimeter.

  1. Reference: measure the LM285-2.5 on the AD7705 module (pin 8 to pin 4); it should read 2.46–2.54 V. Put the value in ADC_VREF in include/config.h and upload.
  2. Raw check: open Calibration and click Raw monitor. In the Console, AIN2 should be about cell voltage ÷ 2, and AIN1 about −10 mV per amp while discharging.
  3. Voltage: load off. Measure the cell at its terminals, enter the reading in Meter voltage, and click Set V.
  4. Current: put the meter (10 A range) in series with the cell lead. Click Load on, wait a few seconds, enter the meter reading in Meter current, click Set I, then Load off. 1–2 A gives the best accuracy.
  5. Click Copy and paste the two #define V_CAL / I_CAL lines into include/config.h, then upload. Without that, the factors are lost at power-off.

Safety

  • Never leave high-current tests unattended. There's no temperature sensing.
  • Use a power-rated load resistor: about V × I watts (4.2 V × 1 A ≈ 4.2 W). It gets hot.
  • Discharge stops at the cutoff voltage. Don't leave a cell connected afterwards: switch SW1 off, because the electronics keep draining it.
  • Only charge cells that aren't damaged, swollen or hot. Use a TP4056 module without the DW01 protection board.
  • Always set the right battery type before testing. Never charge LiFePO4 or LiHV cells with CHG+DIS (it's blocked for them).
